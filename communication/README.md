# Communicate clearly while improving English

English fluency, technical judgement and customer communication are different
skills. Practise them together without treating accent or complex vocabulary as
measures of competence. The goal is understandable, accurate and useful speech.

## Choose the right starting point

- If basic listening or sentence formation is difficult, use level-appropriate
  British Council speaking lessons first, alongside short engineering explanations.
- If you understand English but struggle in meetings, rehearse clarification,
  summaries, disagreement and next-step questions.
- If you speak comfortably but customers remain confused, practise audience,
  evidence, value and decisions. More vocabulary may not solve that problem.

## A daily 20-minute exercise

1. Five minutes: listen to one short lesson, with its transcript where provided.
2. Five minutes: explain one Cedar decision aloud in your own words. First outline
   in your strongest language if helpful, then form short English sentences.
3. Five minutes: listen to your recording or inspect a transcript. Correct one
   confusing sentence and one missing fact. Do not attempt to fix every accent feature.
4. Five minutes: record again and write a three-sentence customer follow-up.

This is a suggested practice routine, not a validated fluency timetable. Get
periodic feedback from a colleague, teacher or intended listener. AI feedback can
be mistaken; use it for rehearsal, not certification.

## Worked example

First attempt: “AI did authorization but backend uncertain.”

Clearer: “The payer received the request. We do not have an authorization decision
   yet. Maya will check the payer status before we send an update.”

What improved: the facts are separated, uncertainty is explicit, and a person owns
the next action. The sentence does not require advanced vocabulary.

## Useful phrases to practise

- “Could you show me the last case where this happened?”
- “Let me check that I understood your concern.”
- “We have tested X. We have not yet measured Y.”
- “The trade-off is lower preparation time against additional review work.”
- “Who needs to agree before we can proceed?”
- “Can we agree the next step, owner and date?”

## Three role-plays

**Discovery:** an operator says every case is different. Learn about one actual
case before proposing a feature. Output: a corrected six-line field note.

**Design review:** a security reviewer rejects the logging plan. Explain the
minimum evidence you need and agree how to restrict access. Output: a revised data flow.

**Commercial objection:** a buyer asks for guaranteed savings before sharing a
baseline. Explain what can be committed now and propose a measurable evaluation.
Output: a bounded proposal and a concrete request for commitment.

An AI may play the fictional customer. Its invented answers are not evidence about
actual customers, policies or market demand. Never practise with confidential
customer content in an unapproved service.

## Self-review rubric

For each item mark absent, partial or clear, and quote the sentence supporting your score:

- Accuracy: did I separate receipt, review and decision?
- Structure: could the listener follow problem, evidence and next action?
- Listening: did I check the other person's meaning?
- Honesty: did I distinguish tested behaviour from assumptions?
- Commitment: did I ask for a specific next step?
- Comprehensibility: could the listener explain my message back?

Do not score accent, social confidence or elaborate vocabulary as engineering ability.

## Legitimate free learning material

- [British Council speaking](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking): level-organized videos and practice activities.
- [British Council Business English](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/business): workplace listening, email and interview practice. Paid teaching services are separate.
- [Google Technical Writing](https://developers.google.com/tech-writing/overview): self-study material for engineering writing. It assumes some English and does not replace a general English course.
- [GOV.UK interviewing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews): practise eliciting evidence rather than leading customers.

The repository supplies original exercises and links to lessons; it does not
redistribute publishers' audio, video or books.
